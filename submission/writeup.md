# Executive Submission Write-Up: The Gilded Ace

Management’s plan to replace the ten lowest-earning slot machines is an expensive mistake. Our investigation of six months of gaming, telemetry, and loyalty data reveals that low house win stems from statistical variance and location starvation—not defective machine math. 

Machine M-1158 illustrates the fallacy: ranked second-lowest in house win ($15,796), it generated $519,610 in coin-in and $51,961 in theoretical win. Its 3.04% observed hold against a 10.0% expected hold reflects a -$36,165 luck residual; players simply enjoyed an extraordinary winning streak. Furthermore, eight of the ten lowest-earning machines sit in the Back Forty, a zone starved of foot traffic. Replacing high-volume games would destroy player loyalty and squander capital.

Tracked loyalty cardholders drive 65.81% of property coin-in ($163.8M) and $15.58M in house win, but exhibit extreme Pareto concentration: the top 5% of players account for 55.33% of coin-in (Gini coefficient: 0.754). Local patrons represent only 7.0% of cardholders yet generate 58.60% of tracked coin-in ($96.0M). 

Despite their importance, cardholders are severely under-rewarded. The casino’s complimentary reinvestment rate is just 1.23% of theoretical win (Las Vegas benchmark: 8%–15%), and points burn rate is an abysmal 4.38%. Because food and beverage economic costs average only 27.93% of menu face value, the house has substantial gross margin to expand comp rewards cheaply.

On the operational side, equipment faults are heavily concentrated: Ridgeline Gaming units caused 56.34% of all breakdowns (680 faults, 2,780 hours downtime). Swing shift suffers 47.8% of faults with only one technician on duty.

For daily coin-in forecasting, a weekday-grouped baseline model trained on January–April predicts May–June demand with superior stability over unadjusted means, confirming strong weekend demand surges that dictate staffing requirements.

We allocate the $100,000 July budget across three targeted, high-ROI actions:
1. **A-COMP-PILOT ($14,000)**: A 1,000-player randomized controlled trial raising dining comp reinvestment to 4.5% of theo for active local players, projected to yield +$13,200 in incremental monthly theoretical win.
2. **A-PREVENTIVE ($9,000)**: A maintenance sweep of all 86 Ridgeline machines during graveyard hours, reducing failures by ~40% and saving ~$3,650 in repair costs.
3. **A-INSPECT ($6,000)**: Paytable verification on the five highest luck-deficit machines to certify EPROM integrity.

We preserve **$71,000** in unspent budget, maintaining financial discipline and capital agility.
